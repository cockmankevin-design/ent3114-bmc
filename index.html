01 — Carried forward

The problem this canvas is built on

Every cell below is downstream of this statement. If the root cause is wrong, the canvas is wrong, and no amount of tidy boxes fixes it.

Problem statement

People buying research peptides online cannot tell which vendors are real not because the lab data is missing, but because every vendor publishes a certificate of analysis and almost none of them can be traced to a specific lot — so the one document buyers were taught to trust has become the cheapest thing in the market to fake.

Root cause

The COA stopped being a verification artifact and became a marketing asset. Nothing stops a vendor from publishing one. No standard says what it has to contain. No third party checks whether the document describes what is actually in the bottle.

A signal that costs nothing to produce carries no information. Buyers are not short of evidence — they are drowning in evidence that cannot be checked.

My unique insight (and why I believe it)

The gap is unverifiable evidence, not missing evidence — and that changes what the fix has to be. It does not require a laboratory. It requires one consistent, published test applied to documents that are already public, and the willingness to refuse to list whoever fails.

I believe it because I went and read them. Vendor after vendor publishes a PDF with a lab logo and no lot number, or reuses one generic report across every product they sell. Nobody applies the test because everyone else in this market earns money from listings, and an exclusion is the one thing a listing business cannot afford.

02 — The canvas

Nine boxes. Nine bets.

Each block is a category of bet, not a description. Every cell below looks reasonable. Every cell could also be wrong — which is the entire point of section 03.

First draft. This is a living document and it updates every time an interview or an audit tells me something real.

Key Partners

Who I bet will help me.

  • Vendor affiliate programmes — the only revenue source, and the supplier of real product feeds: images, prices, stock and tracking links
  • Third-party analytical labs (Janoshik, Colmaric, MZ Biolabs) — as the authors of the reports we read, not as anyone we hire
  • Cloudflare — Workers and KV run the subscriber list on infrastructure I own
  • Moderators of the research communities where buyers already ask "who is real?"

Key Activities

What I bet I'll do.

  • Run the four-gate COA check on every listing, and re-run it when a lot changes
  • Score, re-score, and publicly delist anyone who drops below 7
  • Publish the methodology and every score change
  • Keep the catalogue current from vendor product feeds rather than by hand
  • Write the buyer-education content that earns the search traffic

Key Resources

What I bet I'll need.

  • The published scoring rubric — purity 40%, COA transparency 20%, consistency 20%, price 10%, shipping 10%. This is the actual product
  • Acceptance into affiliate programmes, which gates real prices, images and links
  • The live site and the subscriber list, both on infrastructure I own outright
  • My own build stack — static export, free hosting, no engineering payroll

Value Propositions

What I bet they'll want.

Every vendor publishes a COA. Almost none of them will show you the lot. We check, and we publish who fails.

  • A list where the entry requirement is traceable evidence, not an advertising budget
  • Below 7 out of 10 is not listed. No paid placement, no sponsored tier
  • Safety before price before shipping, in that order, on every page, no exceptions
  • We run no laboratory and commission no testing — we verify evidence that already exists, and we say exactly how
  • Not a vendor: no inventory, no shipping, we never touch product

Customer Relationships

How I bet I'll keep them.

  • A methodology page anyone can audit, and a changelog whenever a score moves
  • An email list that exists to announce delistings and new verifications
  • Answering hard questions in public in the communities — including about our own affiliate revenue
  • No urgency timers, no invented review counts, no fabricated scarcity. Those convert, and they are the exact thing this site exists to argue against

Channels

How I bet I'll reach them.

  • Organic search on high-intent queries: "is [vendor] legit", "[peptide] COA"
  • Reddit and forum threads where the question is already being asked
  • The email list at the moment of a delisting — the most shareable thing we produce
  • Later: short-form video teaching people to read a COA themselves

Customer Segments

Who I bet will buy.

Adults 25–45 already buying research peptides online who have been burned once, or are one bad order away from it. They read COAs, they don't trust them, and they resolve it today by asking strangers on Reddit.

  • Dan, 34. Third order, two vendors. One arrived with a COA whose lot number matched nothing. Spends forty minutes cross-checking every purchase and still isn't sure
  • Not clinicians or patients — a different regulatory universe entirely
  • Not first-time browsers — they have not felt the pain yet

Cost Structure

What I bet it'll cost.

  • Hosting near zero — static export on GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Workers and KV free tier
  • My time running the gate check and re-scoring — the real recurring cost, and the one that scales with every listing added
  • Content production for search
  • Legal review of the disclaimers and the research-use-only positioning
  • No inventory, no shipping, no lab invoices — deliberately

Revenue Streams

How much I bet they'll pay.

  • Affiliate commission on referrals, to verified vendors only
  • Later: clearly marked educational sponsorship that never touches a score
  • Buyers pay nothing, ever — a paywall on safety information defeats the purpose
  • Never pay-to-list and never pay-to-rank. Commission cannot move a score, and the methodology page has to say so in those words

03 — The one that matters

Leap of faith assumption

Of everything on this canvas, this is the bet that ends the business if it comes back wrong. Not "makes it harder" — ends it.

Leap of faith

We believe at least eight of the thirty largest online peptide vendors will both pass all four COA gates and run an affiliate programme that accepts a publisher which publicly delists vendors.

Why it kills the business

The venture only exists in the overlap. A vendor that passes the gates but runs no affiliate programme earns nothing. A vendor with a generous programme that fails the gates cannot be listed, because the 7-point floor is the product. If that overlap is close to empty, there is either nothing worth visiting or no way to earn from it — and no amount of better design or content fixes either one.

How I'd know within two weeks

A spreadsheet, not a product. Thirty vendors, four gates each, scored from their own public pages, plus a column for whether an affiliate programme exists and what its terms say about negative coverage. Then apply to five of them and state plainly in the application what the site does. Fewer than eight in the overlap and the model has to change, not the copy. Cost: two weeks and no money.

04 — The rest of the bets

Key assumptions worth validating

Ordered by what I would test first: cheapest and most fatal at the top. Each one is specific enough that I can picture the experiment that proves it wrong — if I couldn't, it isn't on this list.

01Key PartnersSupply

We believe somewhere between a fifth and half of the thirty largest vendors pass all four gates — enough to build a catalogue, few enough that passing means something.

How I test it

The desk audit. Thirty vendors, four gates, recorded from their own public product pages. No contact with anyone required. Cost: $0 · Time: 2 weeks

What would disprove it

Fewer than five passing leaves the 7-point floor with an empty site. More than twenty-five passing means the gates aren't discriminating and the score carries no signal. Both ends are failures.

02Revenue StreamsModel risk

We believe affiliate programmes will accept and keep a publisher that publicly delists vendors, including vendors inside the same programme.

How I test it

Apply to five programmes and describe the delisting policy in the application itself, not after acceptance. Then read the terms for a clause that allows removal over negative coverage. Cost: $0 · Time: 2 weeks

What would disprove it

Fewer than two acceptances, or terms that forbid negative coverage. Affiliate revenue would then be structurally incompatible with the one thing the site sells.

03Value Propositions

We believe buyers already distrust vendor-published COAs and are doing this verification by hand today — badly, and without knowing what to look for.

How I test it

15 interviews with people who have bought, plus reading 50 recent "is this vendor legit" threads. Count how many raise COAs or lot numbers before I mention either. Cost: $0 · Time: this week

What would disprove it

Buyers choosing on price and shipping speed alone. The insight would be mine and not theirs, and the site would be answering a question nobody is asking.

04Customer RelationshipsIndependence

We believe buyers will still trust the scores after reading, on the methodology page, that we earn commission from the vendors we score.

How I test it

Show two versions of the methodology page to twenty people in the target group, disclosure prominent and disclosure buried, and compare stated intent to click through. Then post the prominent version to one community and read what comes back. Cost: $0 · Time: 1 week

What would disprove it

The disclosure materially cutting intent. That is a revenue-model problem, not a wording problem, and burying it is not an option — independence is the entire product.

05Channels

We believe high-intent search queries carry enough monthly volume to make organic search the primary channel, rather than paid acquisition.

How I test it

Keyword research, then publish three pages written against the exact queries and watch impressions in Search Console for thirty days. Cost: $0 · Time: 30 days

What would disprove it

A few hundred impressions a month. Search would not be the channel, and this becomes a community-led venture with a very different cost structure.

06Customer Segments

We believe the buyer worth serving is the one who has already been burned or nearly burned — not the first-time curious browser.

How I test it

In the same fifteen interviews, split by purchase history and compare how urgently each group describes the problem without prompting. Cost: $0 · Time: this week

What would disprove it

Repeat buyers already having a vendor they trust and refusing to switch. The segment would be first-timers instead, which moves the channel, the content and the whole funnel.

07Cost StructureOperating

We believe one person can run the four-gate check and re-score the whole catalogue in under eight hours a month.

How I test it

Time the desk audit while running it. Thirty vendors once, hours recorded honestly rather than estimated afterwards. Cost: $0 · Time: 2 weeks

What would disprove it

Over twenty hours a month. The catalogue could not grow without either automation or paid help, and the cost structure stops being near-zero.

08Key ActivitiesLegal

We believe publicly scoring and delisting named vendors is defensible when every judgement rests only on that vendor's own published documents.

How I test it

One consult, plus the standing rule already enforced in the codebase: nothing invented is ever published against a real vendor name, and sample figures are marked as sample figures everywhere they appear. Cost: one consult · Time: 1 week

What would disprove it

Counsel advising that comparative scoring of named sellers carries unacceptable risk. The site would publish criteria and pass or fail, and stop publishing a number.
